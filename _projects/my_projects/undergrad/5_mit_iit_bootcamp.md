I was selected as one of **48 participants** from **MIT, the IITs, and the
Asian School of Business (ASB)** for the **MIT-IIT Entrepreneurship Bootcamp**,
organized by the [MIT Sloan School of Management](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/).

The bootcamp was led by [Professor Rajesh Nair](https://rajeshnair.me/),
Professor of the Practice at MIT Sloan, and focused on innovation, design
thinking, entrepreneurship, and rapid prototyping.

Over several weeks at IIT Delhi and IIT Mandi, we worked in
multidisciplinary teams to identify societal problems, conduct field research,
build prototypes, and validate ideas with real users.

Rather than developing a single project, the bootcamp exposed us to a range of
problems across **accessibility, sustainability, healthcare, and social
entrepreneurship.**

---

## Design Thinking in Action

Projects followed an iterative, user-centered process:

**Identify → Research → Ideate → Prototype → Test → Iterate**

We began by understanding problems through field visits and stakeholder
interviews, then developed multiple possible solutions and tested early
prototypes with users.

The emphasis was on learning quickly and validating assumptions, rather
than attempting to build a complete solution from the outset.

# Project Highlights

Throughout the bootcamp, our teams explored problems across accessibility,
sustainability, healthcare, and social entrepreneurship.

## TreatTime

_A pet-powered alarm clock._

Many people struggle to wake up despite multiple alarms.

Our team designed **TreatTime**, a device that dispenses a pet treat at a
scheduled alarm time. The pet's interaction with the device creates an
additional motivation for the owner to wake up.

The prototype explored how **existing daily routines can be leveraged to solve
behavioral problems**.

## Low-Cost Trash Compactor

_Affordable waste management for dense urban environments._

Working with local stakeholders in Delhi, we investigated challenges associated
with municipal waste collection.

We developed a **low-cost trash compactor** using an inexpensive automotive
**scissor jack** as the primary mechanical actuator.

The project demonstrated how readily available components can be repurposed to
create practical engineering solutions.

## Indoor Navigation for the Visually Impaired

_Making unfamiliar environments easier to navigate._

Through interviews with visually impaired users, we learned that navigating
unfamiliar venues independently could be a significant challenge, particularly
during large social gatherings.

Our team explored a **human-assisted navigation system** in which volunteers
could provide real-time voice guidance using existing communication tools.

We validated the concept through live trials and found that continuous audio
assistance improved users' confidence while navigating unfamiliar environments.

The project demonstrated how **existing technologies can be combined to address
accessibility challenges without requiring complex infrastructure**.

## Kamand Coasters

_Supporting rural women entrepreneurs._

In Kamand village, local artisans produced handcrafted coasters, but each
coaster required nearly **two days of manual work**, limiting productivity and
income.

Our team approached the problem from both engineering and business
perspectives:

- Designed a **simple mechanical tool** to streamline the weaving process.
- Developed a **Business Model Canvas** covering pricing, distribution, and
  market access.

The project reinforced that innovation can involve improving both the **product
and the business model surrounding it**.

## BUS (बस)

_Improving accessibility in public transportation._

Public transportation can be particularly challenging for visually impaired
commuters.

Our concept proposed a **voice-first mobile application** that would:

- Guide users to the correct bus stop.
- Provide audio navigation.
- Notify bus drivers when a visually impaired passenger was waiting.
- Assist users in identifying and boarding the correct bus.

The concept focused on improving independence while requiring **minimal changes
to existing public transportation infrastructure**.

# Reflections

The MIT-IIT Entrepreneurship Bootcamp changed how I approach innovation.

The biggest lesson was to **start with people, not technology**. Field visits,
interviews, rapid prototyping, and user feedback helped us uncover problems
that would have been easy to miss from a purely technical perspective.

Across very different projects, the same principle kept emerging: good
solutions come from **testing assumptions, learning from users, and iterating
continuously**.

> **Innovation begins with empathy and succeeds through iteration.**
